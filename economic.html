Economic Impact of the 2026 Iran War

How the conflict is reshaping global energy, trade, and markets — updated as of Day 21

The Cost of War

US Military Spending

  • First 100 hours: $3.7 billion ($891 million/day) — Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS)
  • First 6 days: $11.3 billion — Pentagon
  • Pentagon budget request: $200+ billion — with no defined end date
  • Defense Secretary Hegseth: No "timeframe" for the war's conclusion

Energy Markets

Oil

  • Brent crude: +27% in the first week alone — the biggest weekly gain since the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020
  • Record highs: Oil and fuel cargo prices smashing records by Week 3 (Reuters)
  • Strait of Hormuz: 20% of world's oil passes through — effectively disrupted since Day 1. Iran controlling passage, threatening to fire on US/Israeli ships
  • Kharg Island: Iran's main oil export hub struck by US on Day 14 — Trump claimed it was "totally demolished"
  • IEA assessment: "Greatest energy security threat in history"

Natural Gas

  • Qatar's Ras Laffan: 17% of LNG output cut for up to 5 years after Israeli strike on South Pars gas field (Day 20)
  • Losses: ~$20 billion/year in damage; ~9% hit to Qatar's GDP (Al Jazeera)
  • European gas prices: Surged 25%+ by Day 21
  • Qatar supplies 20% of global LNG — force majeure likely on contracts to Belgium, Italy, South Korea, China

Fuel Prices Worldwide

  • Zimbabwe: Fuel topped $2/litre for the first time — direct result of conflict
  • UK and Europe: Sharp increases across the board
  • US gasoline: Prices rising steadily as war stretches into third week (NPR)
  • Egypt: Costs of distant war driving up local market prices (AP)

Trade and Shipping

Strait of Hormuz

  • Shipping lines rerouted from Day 1 — even before Iran explicitly threatened vessels
  • Iran's new Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei declared strait should remain closed as leverage
  • BBC Verify: Nearly 100 ships have passed through since March 1 — a tiny fraction of normal traffic
  • Red Sea also affected — Houthi movement previously attacked ships there
  • European nations and Japan discussing intervention but no commitments
  • Trump called NATO allies "cowards" for not helping secure the strait

Aviation

  • Dubai International Airport: Damaged by drone strikes on Day 2 — one of the world's busiest airports temporarily halted all flights
  • Middle East flights: Near-complete stop from Day 1, stranding hundreds of thousands
  • Qatar Hamad Airport: Partially resumed via "emergency routes" by Day 7
  • Qatar Airways operating special repatriation flights to European cities

Country-Level Impacts

Oil Producers

  • Kuwait: Began cutting oil production — ran out of storage room (WSJ, Day 8)
  • Russia: "Significant increase in demand" for Russian energy — profiting from disruption. But Lukoil reported $12.4 billion net loss in 2025
  • Iraq: Overtook Russia as India's top oil supplier
  • Serbia: Cutting crude oil excise duties by 60% to calm markets

Importers and Allies

  • Japan: Stockpiling US oil domestically — PM Takaichi announced during White House visit
  • Italy: Seeking gas from US, Azerbaijan, Algeria after Qatari exports halted
  • India: Given 30-day US sanctions waiver on oil imports
  • European Council: Urgently called for moratorium on strikes against energy and water facilities

US Responses

  • Treasury Secretary Bessent: US might "unsanction" Iranian oil already on tankers to ease prices
  • Strategic reserves: Government could release more oil from reserves
  • Trump: Ordered weapons manufacturers to "quadruple" production

Humanitarian Economic Costs

  • Iran: 10,000+ residential homes damaged or destroyed (Tehran governor, Day 16). Infrastructure, factories, and civilian areas hit alongside military targets
  • Lebanon: 1,000,000+ displaced — massive economic burden on a country already in economic crisis
  • Gulf states: Billions in damage to energy infrastructure, airports, and military facilities
  • Qatar GDP: Estimated 9% annual hit from Ras Laffan damage alone
  • Global food supply: Al Jazeera's "Counting the Cost" asks: Could Iran war trigger next global food shock?
  • Pharmaceutical supply chains: Potential disruptions flagged by Think Global Health

The Bottom Line

This is the most significant energy crisis since the 1973 oil embargo — and possibly worse due to the interconnected nature of modern supply chains. The simultaneous disruption of the Strait of Hormuz, direct strikes on oil and gas infrastructure on both sides, and the uncertainty of the conflict's duration have created cascading effects across every sector of the global economy.

The war has cost the US over $11 billion in its first week alone, with a $200+ billion budget request on the table. Meanwhile, oil-importing nations face fuel shortages, inflation spikes, and the prospect of rationing. Countries that can supply alternative energy — US shale producers, Russia, Iraq — are the short-term economic beneficiaries.
