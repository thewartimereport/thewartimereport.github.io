DAY 23 — MARCH 22, 2026 🔴 LIVE — UPDATING

48-Hour Ultimatum — Trump Threatens to Obliterate Iran's Power Grid

The Wartime Report · Published March 22, 2026 · Updated 07:40 UTC · Sources: Al Jazeera, Reuters, CNN, NPR, Axios, CBS, Google News

Summary

The war lurched toward a new and terrifying phase as Trump issued a 48-hour ultimatum: reopen the Strait of Hormuz or the United States will "obliterate" Iran's power plants. Iran responded by threatening to target all US energy infrastructure in the region. The confirmed death toll in Iran has reached 1,444 — including at least 204 children. In Lebanon, renewed Israeli attacks have killed at least 1,001 people including 118 children. Iranian missiles struck near Israel's Dimona nuclear research center and the city of Arad, wounding over 100 — in apparent retaliation for an Israeli attack on the Natanz nuclear enrichment facility. Israel's air defense failed to intercept at least 2 of the incoming strikes. Iran announced its 70th wave of attacks, launching missiles and drones at Israel and US bases in the Gulf. Iran offered a narrow exception: Japanese ships would be allowed to transit Hormuz. Brent crude surged to $112.19 per barrel as Bloomberg warned the "shockwave of war is rippling through the global economy."

Military Developments

Iran Strikes Near Dimona

In the most provocative strike of the war, Iran targeted the area near Israel's Dimona nuclear research center, as reported by Al Jazeera, and the nearby city of Arad, wounding over 100 people. The strikes appeared to be retaliation for an Israeli attack on Iran's Natanz nuclear enrichment facility — raising the specter of a tit-for-tat nuclear infrastructure war that could have catastrophic consequences. Israel's air defense failed to stop at least 2 Iranian missile strikes on southern Israel, a significant failure that will raise urgent questions about Iron Dome and Arrow system capabilities under sustained bombardment.

Netanyahu and IDF Respond

Netanyahu threatened more attacks following the strike on Arad, and the IDF chief approved a new wave of strikes against Iran and Hezbollah. The retaliatory cycle showed no sign of breaking — each side now targeting the other's most sensitive facilities.

Iran Launches Ballistic Missiles at Diego Garcia — Fails

In a dramatic expansion of the war's geographic scope, Iran launched ballistic missiles at the joint US-UK Diego Garcia base in the Indian Ocean. The UK confirmed the attack failed to hit the base. The strike — targeting a facility thousands of kilometers from Iran — signals Tehran's willingness to strike Western military assets far beyond the immediate theater.

IRGC Spokesman Killed in Overnight Tehran Strikes

Overnight US-Israeli air strikes on Tehran killed an IRGC spokesman, whose funeral was held during Eid al-Fitr prayers in the capital. The strikes on Tehran during Eid — Islam's most important holiday — drew widespread condemnation across the Muslim world.

Drone Strike on Iraqi Intelligence HQ in Baghdad

A drone strike near the Iraqi National Intelligence Service headquarters in Baghdad killed a police officer. Iraq attributed the attack to "outlaw groups," but the strike underscored how the war is destabilizing Iraq and drawing in non-state actors.

Projectile Strikes Vessel Off UAE Coast

A projectile struck a vessel off the coast of the UAE, The Guardian reported,, further demonstrating the danger to commercial shipping in Gulf waters. The incident adds to fears that maritime trade in the region is becoming untenable.

Bahrain Patriot System Intercepts Drone

Bahrain confirmed its Patriot missile system intercepted a drone over residential areas, contradicting the US military's earlier account of a March 9 incident that led to civilian injuries. Since February 28, Bahrain reports it has destroyed a total of 143 missiles and 242 drones fired from Iran.

Saudi Arabia and Kuwait Under Fire

Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Defense reported intercepting a massive barrage of drones over its eastern region, shooting down at least 47 drones including a concentrated barrage of 38 within just three hours. Kuwait is also actively "dealing with hostile missile and drone attacks" — its Mina al-Ahmadi refinery, one of the Middle East's largest at 730,000 barrels/day capacity, was hit by two waves of Iranian drones, sparking fires. The Gulf-wide attacks mark a dramatic expansion of Iran's targeting of energy infrastructure.

Iran Threatens "Crushing Blows" to UAE

Iran's military warned it will deliver "crushing blows" to the port city of Ras al-Khaimah if there is any "further aggression" launched from UAE territory against the disputed Gulf islands of Abu Musa and Greater Tunb — raising the prospect of direct Iranian strikes on Emirati cities.

Iran's 70th Wave of Attacks

The Iranian armed forces announced their 70th wave of attacks, launching missiles and drones at Israel and US bases across the Gulf. In Israel, Iran fired nine separate salvoes of missiles; while Israeli air defenses operated to intercept, falling debris and cluster munitions caused widespread damage across central and northern Israel and the western Dead Sea area.

Renewed Attacks on Lebanon — 1,001 Killed

Israel renewed widespread attacks on Lebanon, with at least 1,001 people killed including 118 children since the expanded campaign began, according to Lebanon's Ministry of Public Health. Israel issued evacuation orders for several southern Beirut suburbs — including Haret Hreik, Ghobeiry, and Hadath — before attacks on Hezbollah infrastructure.

US Forces Attacked Near Baghdad Airport

A US logistics support camp near Baghdad's international airport was attacked by rockets, with explosions reported in the area — further evidence that Iraq is becoming an active front in the wider war.

Israel Strikes Syria

Israel hit military infrastructure in southern Syria, claiming it was in response to attacks on Druze civilians in Suwayda. Qatar, Jordan, and Egypt all condemned the strike as a violation of Syrian sovereignty.

Missile Fragments Near Al-Aqsa Mosque

Missile fragments fell in occupied East Jerusalem, just 350 metres from the Al-Aqsa Mosque compound, causing extensive damage to the religiously significant site — an incident certain to inflame tensions across the Muslim world.

Iran Warns of Regional Energy Strikes

Following Trump's ultimatum, Iran warned it would launch strikes on regional energy and water sites if attacked. This threat extended beyond US and Israeli targets to encompass Gulf state infrastructure — a warning that the entire region's civilian population could pay the price for further escalation.

Political Developments

The 48-Hour Ultimatum — But Also "Winding Down"?

In contradictory signals, Trump issued the war's starkest demand yet — Iran must reopen the Strait of Hormuz within 48 hours or the United States will "obliterate" Iran's power plants — while simultaneously saying he was considering "winding down" military operations and that other countries should "guard and police" the strait. He explicitly ruled out a ceasefire. The mixed messaging left analysts and allies confused about US intentions.

Trump Considers Ground Troops — 2,500 More Marines Deploying

CNN and CBS reported that the Trump administration is making "heavy preparations" for the potential deployment of ground troops in Iran — described as Trump's "most difficult war decision yet." The US is deploying an additional 2,200 to 2,500 Marines to the Middle East, with Al Jazeera's correspondent reporting the movements could lead to "potential US boots on the ground in Iran" to secure the Strait of Hormuz. Reuters characterized the war as having "escalated beyond Trump's control" after three weeks.

Trump Mulls Kharg Island Takeover

Axios reported Trump is considering a risky military takeover of Kharg Island — Iran's primary oil export terminal — to force Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Such an operation would represent the first seizure of Iranian sovereign territory and could trigger unpredictable escalation.

Iran's Response

Iran responded with its own threat: if attacked, all US energy infrastructure in the region would be targeted. In a calculated diplomatic move, Iran simultaneously announced it would allow Japanese ships to transit the Strait of Hormuz — an attempt to fracture the coalition by offering Tokyo an escape valve while maintaining the blockade against US-aligned shipping.

EU Urges Gas Stockpiling

The EU urged member states to begin storing winter gas as the Iran war causes "high, volatile" gas prices that threaten European energy security. The warning — issued in March for the following winter — reflects how deeply the conflict is reshaping global energy planning.

Information War Intensifies

Al Jazeera's Listening Post reported that information control is now as critical as the military campaign itself, with Washington, Tehran, and Tel Aviv all tightening narrative management. An Iranian woman's video of a US-Israeli bomb attack — which cuts off as a bomb hits — went viral, highlighting the human cost that official channels are struggling to contain.

Anti-War Protests Grow in US

In Philadelphia, Iran war protesters targeted Senators McCormick and Fetterman, demanding an end to the conflict — a sign that domestic political pressure against the war is building as it enters its fourth week with, as NPR put it, "no clear end in sight."

UK Allows Bases for Iran Strikes

The United Kingdom has allowed the US to use British military bases to carry out attacks on Iranian missile sites — a significant expansion of UK involvement. Iran's Foreign Minister Araghchi responded by warning that British lives were now "in danger", and Iran subsequently launched ballistic missiles at the joint US-UK Diego Garcia base.

Shelter Disparities in Israel

Palestinian citizens of Israel are demanding better protection against incoming missiles, pointing out they do not enjoy the same level of shelter access as other Israeli residents — exposing domestic inequalities amplified by the conflict.

West Bank Violence

As the regional war consumed global attention, violence continued in the occupied territories. Israeli settlers set fire to homes and cars near Jenin, a reminder that the broader Israeli-Palestinian conflict was intensifying in the shadow of the Iran war.

Economic Impact

Bloomberg's headline captured the day's economic reality: "Shockwave of War Is Rippling Through the Global Economy." Brent crude hit $112.19 per barrel as the 48-hour ultimatum sent markets into turmoil. The Panama Canal is now operating at maximum capacity (36-38 vessels daily) due to surging demand for LNG tanker passage as Gulf shipping routes become untenable. Iran's systematic strikes on Gulf energy infrastructure — hitting Kuwait's Mina al-Ahmadi refinery, Saudi facilities, and threatening UAE ports — are disrupting global supply chains far beyond the Strait of Hormuz itself.
