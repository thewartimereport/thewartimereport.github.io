DAY 222 — OCTOBER 8, 2026 LIVE

Pentagon Deploys Hospital Ship to Gulf After Port Shuaiba Strike Kills Six

5 min read · By agrimshar · The Wartime Report · Published October 8, 2026

Summary

The Pentagon moved rapidly to deploy USNS Comfort, one of the Navy's two hospital ships, to the Persian Gulf region following the deadly Iranian drone strike on Port Shuaiba, Kuwait, that killed six US soldiers and wounded dozens more. The deployment came as the shipping industry braced for sustained violence in the Strait of Hormuz after seven ships were attacked in the deadliest week for commercial shipping since the war began. Vice President Vance's demand that Iran halt uranium enrichment as a condition for peace drew swift rejection from Tehran, further dimming prospects for diplomatic resolution.

Military Developments

Hospital Ship Deployed

The Pentagon announced USNS Comfort would deploy to the Persian Gulf within days, signaling expectations of sustained casualties among US forces in the region. The 1,000-bed hospital ship last deployed during humanitarian missions but will now serve as a floating trauma center for coalition forces operating in Kuwait, Bahrain, and UAE bases.

The deployment followed Monday's Port Shuaiba strike, which killed six US soldiers and left dozens wounded at a civilian oil refinery. Kuwait has demanded explanation for why US forces were positioned at the civilian facility at the time of the attack.

Hormuz Violence Continues

After last week's surge in ship attacks — seven vessels hit in seven days — maritime security analysts warned that the Strait of Hormuz has entered a new phase of sustained danger. Coalition naval escorts remain stretched thin, with multiple commercial vessels reporting near-misses from Iranian drone and missile attacks.

Shipping industry sources said war-risk insurance premiums for Hormuz transits have reached record levels, with some insurers refusing coverage regardless of price. The Financial Times reported ship captains are now earning up to $100,000 per month in war-risk bonuses — pro rata — to transit the strait, with junior officers receiving proportional increases.

Political Developments

Iran Rejects Vance Enrichment Demand

Iran swiftly rejected Vice President Vance's demand that Tehran halt uranium enrichment as a precondition for ending the war. Foreign Ministry spokesman Nasser Kanaani called the demand "delusional" and said the US has "no standing" to dictate Iran's nuclear program.

Vance's comments — the clearest articulation yet of US peace terms beyond reopening the Strait of Hormuz — effectively added a new barrier to any diplomatic resolution. Iranian officials said they would never negotiate under military pressure and called the enrichment demand proof that Washington seeks regime change, not peace.

Kuwait Demands Answers

Kuwait summoned the US ambassador to explain why American soldiers were present at Port Shuaiba, a civilian oil refinery, at the time of the Iranian drone strike. Kuwaiti officials said the positioning of US forces at civilian energy infrastructure risked making Kuwait a legitimate military target under international law.

The Pentagon has not yet detailed the circumstances of the strike or clarified whether US forces will be withdrawn from Kuwaiti civilian facilities.

Economic Impact

The sustained Hormuz violence threatens to reverse recent gains in oil exports through the strait. Energy markets responded nervously to reports of continued ship attacks, with Brent crude prices ticking up on fears that shipping companies will avoid the strait regardless of naval escort availability.

The $100,000/month captain bonuses reported by the FT will drive up shipping costs across global energy markets, further straining supply chains already battered by seven months of war. Lower-ranking crew members are receiving proportional war-risk bonuses, multiplying the cost impact across the maritime workforce.

Lloyd's of London raised war-risk premiums for Hormuz transits to record levels, with some insurers refusing to underwrite coverage for any price. Industry sources said the insurance crisis could force more ships to bypass the strait entirely, rerouting around Africa and adding weeks to delivery times.

International Reaction

  • Kuwait — Summoned US ambassador over Port Shuaiba deaths; demanded withdrawal of US forces from civilian facilities
  • Iran — Rejected Vance enrichment demand as "delusional"; accused US of seeking regime change
  • European Union — Renewed calls for ceasefire amid escalating maritime casualties
  • Shipping industry — Lloyd's raised Hormuz war-risk premiums to record levels; some insurers refusing coverage
  • International Maritime Organization — Called emergency session to address Hormuz safety crisis
