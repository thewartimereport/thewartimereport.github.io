DAY 135 — JULY 12, 2026 — COMPLETED

House Legal Team Prepares Response as Constitutional Battle Intensifies

3 min read · By agrimshar · The Wartime Report · Published July 12, 2026 · Updated throughout the day

Summary

Congressional legal counsel is working through the weekend to craft its response to the White House's executive power claims as the August 5 deadline approaches. Multiple amicus briefs from constitutional law scholars are being filed, with both conservative and progressive legal minds weighing in on the scope of wartime presidential authority. Meanwhile, demining operations in the Strait of Hormuz continue ahead of schedule at 81% complete, with full commercial shipping normalization now expected by July 17-19. Energy markets remain calm despite the constitutional uncertainty, with Brent crude steady at $72/barrel.

What to Watch

  • House response deadline — Legal team has until August 5 to file comprehensive response to White House arguments
  • Amicus brief wave — Former administration officials from both parties, constitutional scholars, and civil liberties groups filing friend-of-the-court briefs by July 30
  • Historical precedent debate — Legal experts analyzing whether case more closely resembles United States v. Nixon (executive privilege limits), Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer (steel seizure rejection), or wartime precedents like Hamdi v. Rumsfeld
  • Public interest surge — August 12 oral arguments expected to draw massive media coverage and potential protests outside Supreme Court
  • Decision timeline — Court watchers predict ruling by early-to-mid September, potentially before Labor Day weekend
  • Political ramifications — Ruling could reshape executive-legislative balance for generations, affecting future military actions and national security emergencies
  • Hormuz progress update — Demining now 81% complete, up from 78% yesterday, with commercial shipping expected to fully normalize within week
  • Energy stability — Oil markets showing little reaction to constitutional battle, Brent holding at $72/barrel as shipping normalization continues
