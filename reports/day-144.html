DAY 144 — JULY 21, 2026 — COMPLETED

Shipping Normalization Complete as Asian Ports Clear Final Backlogs

5 min read · By agrimshar · The Wartime Report · Published July 21, 2026 · Updated throughout the day

Summary

Global shipping normalization reached completion Monday as Asian ports cleared the final backlogs accumulated during five months of Hormuz closure and insurance restrictions. Container freight rates on Europe-Asia routes dropped to pre-war levels for the first time since February, while tanker transits through the Strait reached 53 vessels — the highest single-day count since the conflict began. The economic milestone came as both Justice Department and congressional legal teams entered their final preparation phase ahead of August 12 Supreme Court oral arguments.

Shipping Industry Breakthrough

Asian Port Backlogs Eliminated

The Port of Singapore, Shanghai's Yangshan terminal, and Hong Kong's Kwai Tsing container facility all reported zero vessel waiting times Monday morning — the first time all three major Asian hubs have operated at normal capacity simultaneously since February 28. The milestone reflected the combined effect of restored Hormuz capacity and eliminated war risk insurance premiums.

Freight Rates Return to Pre-War Levels

Shanghai Containerized Freight Index readings for Europe-Asia routes returned to February 27 levels, with per-TEU costs declining 62% from their March peaks. Industry analysts attribute the normalization to restored shipping lane capacity, eliminated insurance surcharges, and the completion of delayed voyage backlogs.

Record Strait Transits

Fifty-three tankers passed through the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, surpassing Saturday's previous record and approaching the pre-war average of 55-60 daily transits. The milestone indicated shipping companies' confidence in both physical security and insurance cost normalization following Sunday's IMO certification.

Economic Indicators

Energy and commodity markets reflected sustained normalization:

  • Brent crude — $69/barrel, down from $70 as markets anticipate continued supply increases
  • WTI crude — $65/barrel, tracking Brent's gradual decline
  • Natural gas — $3.05/MMBtu, continued downward pressure from resumed Qatar LNG exports
  • Gold — $4,050/oz, further retreat from wartime safe-haven peaks
  • VIX — 15.2, approaching pre-conflict volatility levels
  • Baltic Dry Index — Up 15% week-over-week on improved dry bulk shipping fluidity

Supreme Court Preparations Intensify

Final Moot Court Sessions

Both legal teams completed intensive final preparation sessions Monday, with Justice Department attorneys conducting mock oral arguments before former Solicitors General and constitutional law scholars. Congressional legal counsel similarly rehearsed responses to anticipated justices' questions on the practical implications of their positions.

Amicus Brief Analysis Begins

With 68 amicus briefs now filed, Court watchers and legal analysts began detailed review of the wide-ranging constitutional arguments presented. The briefs reflect unusual coalitions spanning the ideological spectrum, with progressive civil liberties organizations and conservative limited-government advocates both supporting congressional oversight positions from different constitutional premises.

Roberts' Consensus-Building Efforts Continue

Reports from Court insiders indicate Chief Justice Roberts continues working to build majority support for a narrow ruling that addresses the immediate dispute without establishing broad precedent on wartime executive privilege. The approach mirrors Roberts' pattern in previous high-stakes separation-of-powers cases during his tenure as Chief Justice.

Global Shipping Developments

  • Qatar LNG exports — Resumed full capacity following Ras Laffan repairs and insurance normalization
  • European LNG spot prices — Down 18% from July highs as Qatar capacity returns
  • Container ship deployment — Major carriers announce restoration of pre-war Asia-Europe service frequencies
  • Dry bulk shipping — Iron ore and coal transport rates approaching February baseline levels
  • Tanker charter rates — Declining as vessel supply-demand balance normalizes

What to Watch

  • Brent crude trajectory — Whether prices decline below $70/barrel toward pre-war $72 baseline
  • Retail fuel price response — Lagged consumer price adjustments to lower transportation costs over 2-3 weeks
  • July 30 amicus deadline — Final Supreme Court friend-of-the-court brief submissions
  • Container freight sustainability — Whether pre-war rate levels prove stable or face seasonal adjustment pressures
  • August 12 oral arguments — Final Supreme Court hearing preparation week begins
  • LNG market rebalancing — Asian and European spot price convergence as Qatar supply normalizes
