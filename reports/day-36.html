DAY 36 — APRIL 4, 2026 LIVE

Two US Aircraft Shot Down in Single Day as Iran Proves Air Defenses Intact

8 min read · By agrimshar · The Wartime Report · Published April 4, 2026 · Last updated: April 4, 2026 at 12:12 UTC

Summary

Friday became the worst day for US aviation losses since the early Iraq War. An F-15E Strike Eagle from RAF Lakenheath was shot down over southwestern Iran — the first confirmed US manned aircraft loss of the war. Both crew ejected, but one weapons-systems officer remains missing as Iranian forces and US rescue teams race to reach him. Two search-and-rescue helicopters were damaged during the recovery operation. Then an A-10 Warthog was shot down over the Persian Gulf during the rescue mission. The simultaneous losses shatter Pentagon claims that Iran's air defenses are "degraded" and prove Tehran can still inflict significant casualties despite 36 days of bombardment. Trump's April 6 deadline for Iran infrastructure strikes is now two days away.

Military

  • F-15E Strike Eagle shot down over Iran — first confirmed US manned aircraft loss of the war. One crew member rescued; one weapons-systems officer (WSO) still missing. Iranian state media (IRIB) is broadcasting pleas for citizens to find the missing American, promising "valuable rewards" for handing over pilots alive. (Reuters, CNN)
  • A-10 Warthog shot down over Kuwait — hit during the search-and-rescue mission for the F-15E crew. Pilot ejected and was recovered. (Reuters)
  • Two Blackhawk SAR helicopters hit by Iranian fire during the rescue operation but made it out of Iranian airspace. (CBS News)
  • Iranian missiles hit residential areas in central Israel — cluster munitions deployed over central Israel, striking Rosh HaAyin. One person injured in Tel Aviv. IDF detected additional ballistic attacks targeting central Israel. (Al Jazeera)
  • Israel bombards Beirut as regional escalation widens. (NYT)
  • 13 US bases in Middle East "nearly uninhabitable" after Iranian missile strikes — CENTCOM forced to disperse troops as the war reshapes American force posture across the region. (Defence Security Asia)
  • Iran rapidly repairing missile bunkers — US intelligence says Tehran is reconstituting launch capability faster than expected despite sustained bombardment. (NYT)
  • Iraqi militia claims 19 attacks on "enemy bases" in 24 hours — the Islamic Resistance in Iraq escalating proxy operations. A second airstrike also hit a PMF (Popular Mobilization Forces) base in western Iraq. (Middle East Eye, FPRI)
  • Cluster bomb attack collapses building in Israel — electricity line also damaged in the strike. (Middle East Eye)
  • Hezbollah fires rockets at Israeli position near Maroun al-Ras — southern Lebanon front remains active. (NYT)
  • Sirens sound in Saudi Arabia and Bahrain — Saudi alert later lifted, but the Gulf states are increasingly within range of the expanding conflict. (CBS News)
  • US-Israeli strike hits Bushehr nuclear power plant — one killed — AFP cites Iranian state media confirming the strike on the operational nuclear facility. Iran's UN envoy had warned any attack on Bushehr would be a "war crime." The IAEA had previously found no evidence of direct strikes on nuclear facilities — this appears to be the first confirmed hit on the plant itself. (Economic Times/AFP)
  • Iran executes two opposition members amid war — Abolhassan Montazer and Vahid Baniamerian (banned dissident group) hanged after Supreme Court upheld sentences. Internal repression intensifying alongside external conflict. (Al Jazeera)
  • Rocket strikes homes in northern Israeli city without warning — no alert sirens preceded the attack. (Middle East Eye)
  • Iraq closes Shalamcheh border crossing with Iran — after an airstrike on the Iranian side killed an Iraqi citizen. Security sources confirmed to Reuters. The war is now directly impacting Iraqi civilians and sovereignty. (Reuters)

Political

  • Iran refuses to negotiate — Pakistani mediators report Tehran is unwilling to talk, citing "unacceptable" US conditions. (i24NEWS)
  • US military archbishop says war fails "just war" standard — the Guardian reports the US military's own senior Catholic cleric has publicly challenged the legal and moral basis for the conflict. (The Guardian)
  • US warns citizens to leave Lebanon citing escalating security risks following Israeli strikes on Beirut and the downing of US aircraft. (CNN)
  • Iraq caught between factions — Iraqi leaders face a balancing act as the war exposes deep rifts between those hoping for the end of Tehran's influence and those loyal to the Islamic Republic. (The Guardian)
  • Trump's April 6 infrastructure strike deadline is now 2 days away — the president warned Iran of strikes on bridges and electric power plants if its leaders do not agree to his terms. (Reuters)
  • International law experts allege violations — BBC reports growing legal challenges to the conduct of the war; White House says it is making the region safer. (NYT)
  • Trump briefed on ground troop options — ABC News reports the president was briefed on plans including special forces raid to seize Iran's enriched uranium stockpile and a potential seizure of Kharg Island. Contradicts his earlier "I don't care" about uranium comment. (ABC News, The Guardian)
  • Polymarket under fire for betting on downed pilot's fate — Rep. Seth Moulton (D-MA) ripped the prediction market; Polymarket removed the bet after public outrage. Donald Trump Jr. is an investor in the platform. (The Hill)
  • Hegseth fires Army's top generals mid-war — Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth ousted Army Chief of Staff Gen. Randy George, the Army's most senior general, along with Gen. David Hodne (head of Army Transformation and Training Command) and at least one other general. Officials stunned: "It's insane." The firings destabilize military leadership during active combat operations. (Axios)

Economic

  • Oil prices remain elevated — Brent crude at ~$109–112/barrel (futures), WTI spiked to $111.29. Physical dated Brent surged above $140 on Thursday — highest since 2008. (Bloomberg)
  • Airlines in crisis mode — Asian carriers particularly vulnerable to Hormuz closure, with jet fuel supply chains severely disrupted. (Financial Times)
  • Three ships attempt new Oman coast route through the Strait — two supertankers (Dhalkut, Habrut) and LNG vessel Sohar LNG headed east, potentially testing Iran's blockade enforcement (Bloomberg).
  • US intelligence warns Hormuz chokehold won't ease soon — Reuters reports Iran is unlikely to relent on the strait, prolonging energy market disruption.
  • Trump told countries to "just grab" fuel through Hormuz in his Wednesday speech — a statement that has unnerved allies dependent on the waterway.
  • Five EU finance ministers call for windfall profit tax on energy companies — Spain and four other EU states urging the European Commission to impose a bloc-wide tax, warning that surging oil and gas prices from the war will fuel inflation. Reuters exclusive confirmed by Bloomberg. (Reuters)

What to Watch

  • Fate of missing F-15E crew member — whether Iran captures or the US rescues the WSO will dominate the next 24–48 hours and could escalate the conflict dramatically.
  • April 6 infrastructure strike deadline — Trump's threat to hit Iranian bridges and power plants is now 2 days out. Will he follow through?
  • Iran's missile repair speed — intelligence showing rapid bunker reconstruction challenges the Pentagon's "degraded" narrative.
  • Hormuz shipping tests — the Oman coast route attempt may signal a new phase in the blockade standoff.
  • Diplomatic dead end — with Iran refusing to talk and no back channels working, the escalation ladder has fewer off-ramps.
  • Ground invasion debate — Trump being briefed on special forces options including uranium seizure raid. Military analysts questioning whether a conventional ground invasion is even feasible.
  • Gulf state spillover — Saudi Arabia and Bahrain triggering air raid alerts; the conflict is no longer contained to Iran, Israel, and Lebanon.
  • Bushehr nuclear fallout — the confirmed strike on an operational nuclear power plant is a massive escalation. Russia built Bushehr and may react. IAEA response pending. Environmental and radiological risks unknown.
