DAY 28 — MARCH 27, 2026 🔴 LIVE — UPDATING

Trump Extends Hormuz Deadline to April 6 as Iran Calls Peace Plan 'One-Sided'

The Wartime Report · Published March 27, 2026 · Last updated: March 27, 2026 — 05:00 AM UTC

Summary

Day 28 of the war sees a fragile diplomatic window creak open — then immediately strain under the weight of contradictions. President Trump extended his Hormuz ultimatum by 10 days to April 6, pausing the threat to strike Iran's power grid while claiming talks are going "very well." Iran allowed 10 oil tankers through the Strait of Hormuz as what Trump called a "present" — a goodwill gesture ahead of negotiations. But Tehran simultaneously dismissed the US 15-point peace plan as "one-sided and unfair", and Trump rejected suggestions he was desperate for a deal. The Pentagon is considering sending up to 10,000 additional ground troops to the Middle East, while the IDF chief of staff warned that Israel's military will "collapse in on itself" from fighting on multiple fronts. Brent crude pulled back from $108/barrel after the deadline extension. Yemen's Houthis said there is "no reason" to stop Red Sea shipping attacks.

Military Developments

  • Pentagon eyes 10,000 more troops: The Pentagon is looking at sending up to 10,000 additional ground troops to the Middle East to give Trump more military options even as he weighs peace talks with Tehran, according to the Wall Street Journal.
  • IDF chief warns of collapse: The Israel Defence Forces' chief of staff warned that the military will "collapse in on itself" as it faces increasing demands and a growing manpower shortage while fighting on multiple fronts, according to Israeli media reports.
  • Tangsiri fallout continues: The killing of IRGC Navy commander Alireza Tangsiri by Israel on Day 27 in Bandar Abbas continues to reverberate. Tehran has not confirmed his death. Tangsiri was the architect of the Hormuz blockade and a key proponent of Iran's fast-boat naval strategy. Netanyahu said Israel will continue to strike Iranian targets with "full force."
  • Houthis defiant: Yemen's Houthis said there is "no reason" to halt Red Sea shipping attacks, amid fears that if Trump follows through on threats to seize Iran's Kharg Island, Tehran may ask them to escalate.
  • Ship aground off Qeshm: A Thai-flagged cargo ship that was hit by unknown projectiles in the Strait of Hormuz earlier this month has run aground off Iran's Qeshm Island, according to Iran's semi-official Tasnim news agency.

Political Developments

  • Trump extends Hormuz deadline to April 6: In a Truth Social post, Trump wrote: "As per Iranian Government request, please let this statement serve to represent that I am pausing the period of Energy Plant destruction by 10 Days to Monday, April 6, 2026, at 8 P.M., Eastern Time." He added that "talks are ongoing" and going "very well."
  • Iran rejects peace plan: A senior Iranian official told Reuters that the US 15-point proposal for ending the fighting is "one-sided and unfair."
  • Witkoff confirms Pakistan back-channel: Trump envoy Steve Witkoff told a cabinet meeting there were "strong signs" that Tehran was ready to negotiate, publicly confirming for the first time that Washington had passed a 15-point "action list" to Tehran through Pakistani intermediaries.
  • Iran allows 10 tankers through Hormuz: Trump said Iran allowed 10 oil tankers to pass through the strait as a "present" to show it was serious about negotiations.
  • Trump denies desperation: "I read a story today that I'm desperate to make a deal," Trump told reporters. "I'm the opposite of desperate. I don't care."
  • War crimes warnings: International human rights experts and UN officials said the threat to strike power plants is an open threat to possibly commit a war crime.

Economic Impact

  • Brent crude at ~$108: Oil prices rose to their highest level of the week, with Brent crude trading at roughly $108/barrel after Trump's cabinet meeting, before pulling back following the deadline extension announcement.
  • Wall Street's biggest loss of the war: US markets suffered their largest single-day decline since the war began, as uncertainty over the conflict's trajectory weighed on investor sentiment.
  • 10 tankers through Hormuz: The passage of 10 oil tankers through the strait — the first significant easing since Iran closed the waterway — offered a brief signal of de-escalation, though the blockade remains functionally in place.

International Reaction

  • Pakistan confirms intermediary role: Pakistan's foreign minister publicly confirmed the country is relaying messages between Washington and Tehran and that Iran is deliberating on the US proposal.
  • NATO strained: NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said European leaders could not be expected to quickly assist in the Persian Gulf because they were not given advance notice of the Feb. 28 attack on Iran.
  • UN war crimes concern: International human rights experts and UN officials continued to raise alarms about the threat to civilian infrastructure, calling Trump's power grid ultimatum a potential war crime.

What to Watch

  • April 6 countdown begins: Trump's extended deadline for Iran to reopen Hormuz before US strikes on power grid infrastructure — 10 days and counting
  • 10,000 troop deployment: Whether the Pentagon moves forward with sending additional ground forces, signaling escalation even as diplomacy proceeds
  • Iran's response to 15-point plan: Tehran has rejected the plan as "one-sided" but Pakistan says Iran is still deliberating — any counter-proposal could shift dynamics
  • IDF manpower crisis: The chief of staff's warning about military collapse could force Israel to scale back operations on one or more fronts
  • Tangsiri succession: Who replaces the IRGC Navy commander and whether it changes the Hormuz blockade's operational posture
  • Houthi Red Sea escalation: The group's refusal to stand down threatens to widen the war's economic impact beyond the Gulf
  • Oil markets: Brent at $108 — reacting to the extended deadline, tanker passage, and persistent uncertainty
