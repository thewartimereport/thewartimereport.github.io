DAY 145 — JULY 22, 2026 — LIVE

Supreme Court Legal Teams Enter Final Three-Week Sprint Before Oral Arguments

5 min read · By agrimshar · The Wartime Report · Published July 22, 2026 · Updated throughout the day

Summary

Justice Department and congressional legal teams entered the final three-week preparation phase Tuesday as the August 12 Supreme Court oral arguments on executive privilege approached. Both sides conducted intensive moot court sessions and began detailed analysis of 68 amicus briefs while Chief Justice Roberts continued building consensus for a narrow ruling. Global shipping normalization held steady with container freight rates remaining at pre-war levels and Hormuz tanker transits maintaining elevated volumes.

Supreme Court Preparations Accelerate

Final Preparation Phase Begins

With three weeks remaining before oral arguments, both legal teams intensified preparation efforts Tuesday. Justice Department attorneys conducted additional moot court sessions before former Solicitors General and constitutional law scholars, focusing on anticipated questions about the practical implications of broad executive privilege claims during active military operations.

Congressional Strategy Refinement

Congressional legal counsel completed rehearsal sessions addressing justices' likely concerns about oversight mechanisms that preserve congressional authority without compromising operational security. The team refined arguments emphasizing the distinction between real-time tactical decisions and broader strategic oversight of war planning and authorization.

Amicus Brief Analysis Deepens

With all 68 friend-of-the-court briefs now filed, legal analysts continued detailed review of the constitutional arguments spanning the ideological spectrum. The briefs reveal unusual coalitions supporting congressional oversight from different constitutional premises, potentially giving the Court multiple pathways toward a narrow ruling that addresses the immediate dispute without establishing sweeping precedent.

Economic Indicators

Energy and commodity markets reflected continued normalization:

  • Brent crude — $68/barrel, edging closer to pre-war $72 baseline
  • WTI crude — $64/barrel, tracking Brent's gradual decline
  • Natural gas — $3.02/MMBtu, continued downward pressure
  • Gold — $4,035/oz, further retreat from wartime peaks
  • VIX — 15.0, approaching pre-conflict levels
  • Baltic Dry Index — Maintained recent gains on improved bulk shipping fluidity

Shipping Normalization Holds

Asian Ports Maintain Zero Backlog

Major Asian shipping hubs maintained zero vessel waiting times for the second consecutive day Tuesday, confirming Monday's normalization milestone reflected sustainable operational capacity rather than temporary fluctuation. Singapore, Shanghai's Yangshan terminal, and Hong Kong's Kwai Tsing facility all reported normal operations throughout the day.

Freight Rates Stable

Shanghai Containerized Freight Index readings held at pre-war levels Tuesday, with per-TEU costs remaining 62% below March peaks. Industry analysts expressed growing confidence that the normalization represents a lasting structural shift rather than temporary market movement.

Hormuz Transits Remain Elevated

Fifty-one tankers passed through the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, maintaining the elevated transit volumes that indicate sustained shipping industry confidence in both physical security and insurance cost normalization.

Roberts' Consensus-Building Continues

Court insiders report Chief Justice Roberts remains focused on building majority support for a narrow ruling that addresses the immediate executive privilege dispute without establishing broad precedent on wartime presidential powers. The approach would follow Roberts' established pattern in previous high-stakes separation-of-powers cases, seeking to resolve the specific constitutional question while preserving flexibility for future circumstances.

Global Shipping Developments

  • Qatar LNG exports — Maintained full capacity following complete normalization
  • European LNG spot prices — Continued decline from July highs
  • Container ship deployment — Carriers implementing restored pre-war service frequencies
  • Tanker charter rates — Further decline as vessel supply-demand balance normalizes
  • War risk insurance premiums — Maintained elimination following IMO certification
