DAY 30 — MARCH 29, 2026 LIVE — UPDATING

Houthis Enter War as Trump's April 6 Deadline Looms

8 min read · The Wartime Report · Published March 29, 2026 · Last updated: March 29, 2026 — 5:55 PM UTC

Summary

One month into the war, Day 30 brought some of the heaviest Israeli strikes yet as the IDF bombarded Tehran with "widespread" airstrikes targeting weapons production and nuclear sites. Iran fired back at Israel and Gulf allies. Meanwhile, 2,500 US Marines arrived in the Middle East equipped for amphibious assault as the Pentagon prepares for weeks of ground operations. Diplomats from Saudi Arabia, Egypt, and Turkey gathered in Islamabad seeking an end to the conflict, but Iran's parliamentary speaker accused Trump of planning a ground invasion under cover of diplomacy. The death toll in Iran has reached at least 3,461 killed including 1,551 civilians and 236 children. Brent crude at $112.57/barrel. Hormuz still blockaded with ~3,000 ships waiting. Trump's April 6 deadline is eight days away.

Military Developments

  • IDF launches "widespread" strikes on Tehran: Israel bombarded Tehran with an intense wave of airstrikes on Sunday, targeting weapons production facilities, command centers, and air-defense systems. Saturday saw 701 recorded strikes — three-quarters in Tehran alone — among the heaviest single-day bombardment since the war began. The IDF confirmed it is now prioritizing weapons and nuclear sites.
  • 2,500 US Marines arrive for amphibious assault: An expeditionary force of 2,500 Marines arrived in the Middle East attached to ships equipped for amphibious assault, as the Pentagon prepares for weeks of ground operations. Trump has not yet approved. Iran's parliament speaker Ghalibaf said Iranian forces are "waiting for the arrival of American soldiers on the ground to set fire to them."
  • Houthis launch second attack in 24 hours: Yemen's Houthis fired a second barrage of missiles at Israel, with their spokesman vowing attacks "until the aggression ends." Cluster munitions landed in Savyon, Israel, with shrapnel damage to residential areas.
  • Netanyahu expands Lebanon invasion: Netanyahu ordered an expansion of the Lebanon buffer zone on Sunday, vowing to "fundamentally change the security situation in the north." The IDF says 850 Hezbollah fighters killed, many from the elite Radwan Force.
  • IRGC threatens US universities — Monday deadline: The IRGC issued an ultimatum: the US must officially condemn strikes on Iranian universities by noon Monday March 30 (Tehran time) or face retaliation against American universities across the region. The American University of Beirut switched to fully online classes Monday-Tuesday "out of an abundance of caution."
  • Russia transferring drones to Iran: The Associated Press reported "very active" Russia-Iran discussions about transferring upgraded drones, with UK intelligence confirming Russia has already provided drone training, intelligence, and electronic warfare support.
  • Bahrain intercepts 391 drones, 174 missiles: Bahrain's total since Feb 28: 391 drones and 174 ballistic missiles intercepted. Alba, the country's largest aluminum smelter, confirmed an Iranian attack struck its facilities.
  • Prince Sultan Air Base hit: About two dozen US service members wounded at Saudi Arabia's Prince Sultan Air Base, with two seriously injured and two military planes damaged — one of the most serious breaches of US defenses since the war began.
  • Casualty toll: Iran: at least 3,461 killed (1,551 civilians, 1,208 military, 236 children) per HRANA. Iran's Health Ministry separately confirmed 244 women and 231 children killed. Israel: 16+ killed in Iranian attacks. Lebanon: 1,110+ killed. Gulf states: 50+ killed. US: 13 KIA, 300+ wounded.

Political Developments

  • Pakistan peace talks underway: Foreign ministers from Saudi Arabia, Egypt, and Turkey convened in Islamabad on Sunday for mediation talks. But there was little sign that US or Iranian officials would attend. Iran allowed 20 Pakistani-flagged ships through Hormuz (two per day) — a diplomatic gesture, though the blockade remains in force for all others.
  • Iran rejects 15-point plan: Iran's parliament speaker Ghalibaf publicly accused Trump of using diplomacy as cover for a ground invasion: "America expresses its desires and announces what it has not achieved in the war as a list of 15 points." He declared: "As long as the Americans seek Iran's surrender... we will not accept humiliation."
  • Internal regime fractures: Iran's President Pezeshkian is reportedly clashing with IRGC chief Vahidi over the war's economic and social impact — the most visible rift within Tehran's leadership since the conflict began.
  • Iran's ambassador defies Lebanon expulsion: Iran's ambassador to Lebanon refused to leave despite being declared persona non grata with a Sunday deadline. He stayed at the request of parliament speaker Nabih Berri and Hezbollah. Hezbollah ministers boycotted a cabinet session in protest.
  • Trump slams NATO: Trump called NATO a "paper tiger," accusing allies of failing to support the US. Germany's Chancellor Merz countered that "regime change" through war has "mostly gone wrong."
  • Three Lebanese journalists killed: Three journalists — including Al Manar presenter Ali Choeib and two Al-Mayadeen reporters — were killed in an Israeli airstrike. Mourners gathered in Beirut on Sunday. The IDF claimed Choeib was a Hezbollah armed wing member.
  • Israeli anti-war protests grow: Hundreds gathered in Tel Aviv and other cities for unauthorized demonstrations — growing from dozens to hundreds weekly since February 28.
  • Palm Sunday blocked in Jerusalem: Israeli police barred senior Christian leaders from entering the Church of the Holy Sepulchre for Palm Sunday Mass and cancelled the traditional procession due to wartime bans on public gatherings and missile debris risk. All Old City holy sites — including Al Aqsa Mosque and the Western Wall — have been closed since February 28.

Economic Impact

  • Oil at war high — WTI touches $100: Brent crude at $112.57/barrel (+4.22%); WTI surged to $99.64, briefly crossing $100 intraday for the first time since 2022. Dubai physical crude at $126/bbl. Goldman Sachs estimates $14-18/bbl geopolitical risk premium; JPMorgan flagging $120 as the risk scenario. Prices up 51%+ since the war began.
  • Iran's yuan toll system: Iran has turned Hormuz into a yuan-denominated "toll booth" — rerouting select Chinese, Russian, and allied vessels through an alternate path near Qeshm and Larak islands while collecting fees in Chinese yuan. Pakistan separately negotiated passage for 20 ships (2/day). A de-dollarization milestone at the world's most critical oil chokepoint.
  • Gulf infrastructure targeted: Iran's attack on Bahrain's Alba aluminum smelter signals targeting of economic infrastructure beyond military assets. Combined with Bahrain's total intercept count (391 drones, 174 missiles), Gulf states face sustained economic disruption.
  • April 6 energy threat: Trump's ultimatum to bombard Iranian power plants unless Hormuz reopens is eight days away. The NYT notes such attacks could affect millions of civilians and potentially constitute a war crime under the Geneva Conventions.

International Reaction

  • Russia deepens involvement: Beyond drone transfers, Russia is reportedly providing satellite intelligence via the Liana spy satellite system, giving Iran data on US military positions. UK intelligence confirms Russia has provided drone training, EW support, and intelligence sharing.
  • Four-nation mediation in Islamabad: Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Turkey, and Pakistan are actively coordinating, but neither the US nor Iran sent officials to Sunday's talks — raising questions about whether diplomacy can gain traction while bombing intensifies.
  • Germany rejects regime change: Chancellor Merz publicly questioned the war's goals, saying regime change through military force has historically failed.
  • IAEA urges restraint: The UN nuclear watchdog repeated calls for restraint as the IDF confirms nuclear sites are now a priority target.
  • Houthi threat to global shipping: The NYT notes that Houthi entry into the war could further ensnarl global shipping — already stressed by Hormuz — by threatening Red Sea passage, as they did during the Gaza conflict.

What to Watch

  • Ground invasion decision: 2,500 Marines on station. Pentagon plans ready. Iran says it's "waiting." Trump's decision could come at any time.
  • April 6 Hormuz deadline: Eight days until Trump's ultimatum to bomb Iranian power plants — an act the NYT warns could be a war crime
  • IRGC university deadline: Monday noon Tehran time — will the US respond, and will Iran follow through?
  • Pakistan talks: Can four-nation mediation gain traction without US or Iranian officials present?
  • Houthi + Red Sea: Two attacks on Israel in 24 hours — renewed Red Sea shipping disruption would compound the Hormuz crisis
  • Iran internal fractures: Pezeshkian vs IRGC power struggle could reshape war strategy and openness to talks
