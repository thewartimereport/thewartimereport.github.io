DAY 26 — MARCH 25, 2026 🔴 LIVE — UPDATING

Iran Dismisses Trump's Peace Claims as Strikes Kill 12 in Tehran

The Wartime Report · Published March 25, 2026 · Last updated: March 25, 2026 — 3:15 PM UTC

Summary

Day 26 brought an escalating gap between rhetoric and reality. President Trump declared the "war has been won" and claimed VP Vance, Secretary Rubio, Witkoff, and Kushner were all participating in talks with Iran — but Tehran's military forcefully dismissed any negotiations. Pakistan offered to host peace talks and reportedly delivered a US 15-point plan addressing Iran's nuclear and missile programs and Strait of Hormuz shipping. On the ground, Israeli warplanes launched new strikes on Tehran government infrastructure, Iran hit back with missiles toward Israel, and the fighting expanded into Lebanon with Israeli jets roaring over Beirut and renewed evacuation warnings for Dahiya. The Pentagon ordered ~2,000 troops from the 82nd Airborne deployed — bringing total additional ground forces in the region to nearly 7,000. Oil plunged 6% to $94/barrel on the diplomatic signals. Iran's UN ambassador has reported at least 1,348 civilians killed since Feb 28; independent monitors put the toll above 1,440. Over 1,000 have died in Lebanon.

Military Developments

  • Tehran struck: US-Israeli airstrikes hit southern Tehran, killing at least 12 and wounding 28, according to Iranian state media.
  • Mutual airstrikes: Israel and Iran exchanged airstrikes on Wednesday, continuing the pattern of escalatory tit-for-tat strikes.
  • Iran missile wave: Iran fired a new wave of missiles targeting Israel and Gulf states. A CNN team on the ground witnessed what appeared to be cluster munitions from an Iranian launch.
  • Gulf under fire: Saudi Arabia intercepted at least 30 drones since Tuesday evening; Kuwait intercepted 6, with drones hitting a fuel tank at Kuwait international airport causing a fire; Bahrain sounded air-raid sirens early Wednesday (NYT/Guardian).
  • 82nd Airborne deployment: Pentagon ordered ~2,000 soldiers from the 82nd Airborne to the Middle East, bringing total additional ground forces in the region to nearly 7,000 (NYT, two DoD officials).
  • Beirut strikes: Israeli warplanes roared low over Beirut until dawn; renewed evacuation warnings issued for Dahiya (southern suburbs), a Hezbollah stronghold. Lebanon's NNA confirmed strikes around 4:30 AM (NYT).
  • Israel targets Tehran infrastructure: Israeli military announced new wave of strikes on government infrastructure in Tehran (NYT).
  • Lebanon occupation plan: Israel announced plans to occupy parts of southern Lebanon (Reuters).
  • USS Abraham Lincoln targeted: Trump claimed Iran fired more than 100 missiles at the USS Abraham Lincoln carrier, saying "every single one of them was knocked down" (NYT, 8:01 AM ET).

Political Developments

  • Trump: "War has been won": Trump declared victory and named VP Vance, Secretary Rubio, Steve Witkoff, and Jared Kushner as participating in Iran talks (CNN/NYT).
  • 🔴 Iran REJECTS 15-point plan: Iran officially rejected the US ceasefire proposal delivered via Pakistan. Press TV quoted an anonymous official: "Iran will end the war when it decides to do so and when its own conditions are met." Iran issued its own counterproposal demanding reparations and sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz (AP).
  • Iran counterproposal: Tehran's counter-demands include reparations for war damage, affirmed sovereignty over Hormuz, and continued support for regional allies — all nonstarters for Washington.
  • Friday talks still possible: Despite the rejection, mediators (Egypt, Pakistan) are pushing for possible in-person US-Iran talks as soon as Friday in Pakistan (AP, citing Egyptian and Pakistani officials).
  • 15-point plan details: The US proposal addressed sanctions relief, rollback of Iran's nuclear program, missile limits, restrictions on support for armed groups, and reopening the Strait of Hormuz (AP, Pakistani and Egyptian officials).
  • Senate rejects war powers: The US Senate voted down a war powers resolution that would have required congressional authorization for continued military action against Iran.
  • AP-NORC poll: Most Americans say US military action against Iran has gone too far (AP).
  • Pakistan mediation: Pakistan offered to host peace talks; PM Sharif said his country was ready. Pakistan delivered the US 15-point plan to Tehran. A senior Iranian official told Reuters that either Pakistan or Turkey could be venues for de-escalation discussions.
  • China engagement: China's FM Wang Yi spoke with his Iranian counterpart at Tehran's request, saying he hoped "all sides will seize every opportunity" for peace. China is the leading buyer of Iranian oil (NYT).
  • Hormuz partial opening: Iran told the UN maritime organization that "non-hostile" ships may pass through the Strait of Hormuz — defined as those not participating in or supporting aggression against Iran, and not belonging to the US or Israel (NYT).
  • March 28 deadline: Trump's ultimatum for Iran to fully reopen Hormuz is now 3 days away.

Economic Impact

  • Oil plunges on peace signals: Brent crude fell 6% to $94/barrel on Pakistan mediation news, its sharpest single-session drop since the war began. Asian stock markets opened higher on de-escalation hopes (NYT).
  • Brent crude: Swinging between $94–$104/barrel, extremely sensitive to every diplomatic signal.
  • WTI crude: Approximately $87/barrel.
  • Hormuz context: Asia buys ~80% of oil shipped through the Strait of Hormuz — any opening, even partial, has outsized market impact.
  • First tanker transit: Thai energy company Bangchak confirmed a tanker anchored in the Persian Gulf since March 11 has safely transited the Strait of Hormuz under Iran's "non-hostile" ship policy — the first known transit since the war began. The vessel is crossing the Indian Ocean with crude oil due in Thailand in early April (Guardian).
  • Philippines energy emergency: President Marcos Jr. declared a national energy emergency, citing "imminent danger of critically low energy supply." The government is distributing fuel subsidies and bracing for possible evacuation of 2.4 million Filipinos in the Middle East (AP).

What to Watch

  • March 28 Hormuz deadline: 3 days remain — will Iran's partial "non-hostile ships" opening satisfy Trump, or escalate pressure?
  • Friday talks in Pakistan: Iran rejected the 15-point plan but mediators still pushing for in-person talks Friday. Will Iran show up?
  • China's role: Beijing's engagement at Tehran's request could signal Iran seeking an exit ramp — or leverage.
  • Massive troop buildup: 1,000+ paratroopers (82nd Airborne) + 5,000 Marines + thousands of sailors deploying — amphibious assault capability now in theater.
  • Casualty toll: At least 1,348–1,440 Iranian civilians and 1,000+ in Lebanon killed — humanitarian pressure mounting.
  • Lebanon front: Beirut strikes and Dahiya evacuation warnings suggest active escalation — second ground front possible.
