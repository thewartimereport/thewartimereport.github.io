DAY 223 — OCTOBER 9, 2026 LIVE

USNS Comfort Arrives as Hormuz Casualties Mount

5 min read · By agrimshar · The Wartime Report · Published October 9, 2026

Summary

USNS Comfort reached the Persian Gulf on Thursday as sustained maritime violence in the Strait of Hormuz drove the shipping insurance market to crisis. Lloyd's of London formally declared the strait uninsurable at any premium, forcing shipping companies to choose between self-insuring multi-billion-dollar assets or avoiding the waterway entirely. Kuwait continued to press the Pentagon for repositioning US forces away from civilian energy facilities following the Port Shuaiba strike that killed six US soldiers. Ship captains are now demanding bonuses exceeding $100,000 per month for Hormuz transits.

Military Developments

Hospital Ship Reaches Gulf

USNS Comfort arrived in the Persian Gulf region Thursday afternoon, positioning off Bahrain to provide floating trauma care for US and coalition forces. The 1,000-bed hospital ship will serve Kuwait, Bahrain, and UAE bases amid expectations of sustained casualties from Iranian drone and missile attacks.

The ship's arrival followed Monday's Port Shuaiba strike that killed six US soldiers and wounded dozens at a Kuwaiti oil refinery. Pentagon officials said Comfort will remain on station indefinitely, acknowledging that the Strait of Hormuz maritime campaign shows no signs of de-escalation.

Kuwait Demands Force Repositioning

Kuwait continued diplomatic pressure on Washington to relocate US forces from civilian energy facilities. Foreign Ministry officials warned that positioning troops at oil refineries makes Kuwait a legitimate military target under international law and risks drawing the emirate deeper into the conflict.

The Pentagon has not yet responded to Kuwait's demand for repositioning. US Central Command officials privately acknowledged the Port Shuaiba positioning was intended to deter Iranian strikes on Kuwaiti energy infrastructure — a strategy that evidently failed.

Economic Impact

Lloyd's Declares Hormuz Uninsurable

Lloyd's of London formally declared the Strait of Hormuz uninsurable Thursday, refusing to underwrite war-risk coverage for any premium after sustained Iranian attacks on commercial shipping. The declaration effectively forces shipping companies to self-insure vessels transiting the strait or avoid the waterway entirely.

Industry analysts said the Lloyd's decision will accelerate the shift toward Cape of Good Hope routing, adding weeks to delivery times and billions in annual shipping costs. Several major European and Asian shipping companies have already announced temporary suspensions of Hormuz transits pending reassessment of naval escort effectiveness.

Captain Bonuses Exceed $100K

Ship captains are now demanding war-risk bonuses exceeding $100,000 per month for Hormuz transits, with junior officers receiving proportional increases. The Financial Times reported that some shipping companies are paying $120,000 monthly premiums to retain qualified captains willing to transit the strait.

Maritime labor unions said the bonuses reflect genuine risk — seven ships attacked in seven days, with multiple casualties among crew members. Industry sources said the premium structure is unsustainable and will force more carriers to bypass Hormuz regardless of cargo urgency.

  • International Maritime Organization — Called emergency session Friday to address Hormuz insurance crisis
  • Iran — No official comment on Lloyd's decision; Iranian media framed it as economic victory
  • Japan — Warned of energy supply disruptions if Hormuz remains uninsurable
